## 2. Why the single-trace route fails

The marginality computation is decisive:

[
\rho^{2m}\le \dim\cdot \Delta_s^m.
]

At (s=0),

[
\Delta_0=\frac13,
]

and with (\dim\sim 3^c), taking (m\sim c) gives

[
\rho^2\le 3^{c/m}\Delta_0.

At the pigeonhole floor (m\sim c),

[
3^{c/m}\Delta_0\sim 3\cdot \frac13=1.

So the single-trace method has exactly zero spare margin.

This is not a technical defect. It is structural. It explains why the observed radii approach

[

from above rather than collapsing below it with an obvious gap. The entropy loss and the dimension growth exactly balance.

So any proof of the gap must exploit **higher-order chain structure**, not one trace.

## 4. The live proof route: renewal Fourier

The renewal-Fourier route is the right one. The random variable is not the full word (w); it is the constant

[
C_w \bmod 3^c.
]

It evolves by the recursion

[
C_{wb}
======

3C_w+2^{B_m(w)}
]

or, more explicitly, if you track

[
(C_w, B_m(w))\pmod{3^c,\varphi(3^c)},
]

then appending (b) gives

[
B\mapsto B+b,
]

[
C\mapsto 3C+2^B.
]

So the natural Markov state is

[
(C,B)\in \mathbb Z/3^c\mathbb Z\times \mathbb Z/\varphi(3^c)\mathbb Z,
]

with branch law

[
p_s(b)\propto 2^{-(1+s)b}.
]

The Fourier coefficient of the (C)-marginal is

[
\widehat\mu_m(u)
================

\mathbb E\left[
e!\left(\frac{u C_w}{3^c}\right)
\right].
]

The recursion is

[
\widehat\mu_{m+1}(u,\xi)
========================

\sum_b p_s(b),
e!\left(\frac{u2^B}{3^c}\right)
\cdot
\widehat\mu_m(3u,\xi+b)
]

after introducing the dual variable for (B). More invariantly, one needs the transfer operator on Fourier modes ((u,\xi)).

This is exactly where the damping lemma reappears: the branch average over (b) gives factors of the form

[
\frac{1-q}{1-q\zeta},
]

and non-coboundary modes are damped unless they fall into the quadratic-parity coboundary sector.

So the wrap lemma should become a **Fourier flattening theorem** for the renewal distribution of (C_w\bmod 3^c).

## 8. How to prove the renewal flattening lemma

The state recursion

[
C_{j+1}=3C_j+2^{B_j},
\qquad
B_{j+1}=B_j+b_j
]

suggests a two-coordinate Markov chain. The multiplier

[
e(u2^{B_j}/3^c)
]

oscillates as (B_j) moves under the geometric increments (b_j).

Since (2) is primitive modulo (3^c), the map

[
B\mapsto 2^B
]

identifies the (B)-coordinate with (U_{3^c}). Thus the process is a multiplicative random walk on (U_{3^c}) feeding an affine renewal sum.

The branch increment distribution has Fourier transform

[
\hat p_s(\xi)
=============

\frac{1-r}{1-r e(\xi)},
\qquad r=2^{-(1+s)}.
]

Nontrivial frequencies are damped except near (\xi=0), and the only exact obstruction is the quadratic/parity sector already classified.

The proof route:

1. Decompose (u) by conductor:
[
v_3(u)=j.
]

2. The term
[
e(u2^{B_j}/3^c)
]
is a nontrivial character on (B_j) at conductor (3^{c-j}).

3. Over (O(c-j)) steps, the distribution of (B_j) under geometric increments should flatten enough to damp this character.

4. Sum over conductor levels.

This should give either constant or exponential-in-(c) damping of (F_m(u)).

This is a classical-looking Fourier/renewal proof and no longer needs any Collatz-specific global dynamics.

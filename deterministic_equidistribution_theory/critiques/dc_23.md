## 3. Define the scaled cascade operator precisely

The scaled recurrence you found is exactly the right coordinate:

[
\bar t_{i+1}=3\bar t_i+\bar x,
]

with local weight

[
\frac13,2^{-M_{i+1}|\bar x|}.
]

This should become the canonical definition of (\mathcal K_c).

A good state space is:

[
v_i=(i,\bar t_i,\hat r_i),
]

where:

* (i) is the cascade level;
* (\bar t_i) is the scaled locked difference class, i.e.
[
B_i-B_i'=2\cdot3^{c-3-i}\bar t_i;
]
* (\hat r_i) is the target/heavy-residue data needed to decide whether the transition is boundary or deeper.

Then transitions are indexed by the reduced increment

[
\bar x
]

and have weight

[
w_i(v\to v')
============

\frac13,2^{-M_{i+1}|\bar x|},\beta(v,\bar x),
]

with

[
\beta(v,\bar x)\in\left{\frac12,1\right}.
]

The normalized operator should be chosen so that the crude marginal model has Perron radius (1). Then the theorem is meaningfully:

[
\rho(\mathcal K_c)\le1-\delta.
]

Make the normalization explicit. Otherwise a reader will not know whether (\rho(\mathcal K_c)<1) is structural or just a consequence of scaling choices.

## 5. The three proof lemmas are exactly right

The proof should now be organized around these three lemmas.

### Lemma A: (\beta=1) channel bound

You already have:

[
p_1\le K/3<0.733
]

from adjacent-depth cascade mass. That proves a strict saving, but it is much weaker than the observed

[
p_1\approx0.388.
]

The remaining constant (q) is the degenerate boundary channel. The natural target is:

[
q\le \frac13.
]

This should be attacked directly. The (\lambda=0) condition should be one nontrivial congruence modulo (3) on the leading cascade digit. If so, it costs exactly one mod-(3) condition:

[
\Pr(\lambda=0\mid \text{boundary cascade})\le\frac13.
]

Try to prove it row-wise, not just averaged:

[
\Pr(\lambda=0\mid v)\le\frac13
]

for every recurrent cascade state (v). If row-wise fails, prove it after one transition:

[
\Pr(\lambda_{i+1}=0\mid v_i)\le\frac13+O(3^{-i})
]

or prove a two-step version.

This lemma is the key local saving.

### Lemma B: off-representative mass is (o(1))

The data suggest something much stronger than (o(1)):

[
3.6\cdot10^{-3},\ 8.0\cdot10^{-4},\ 1.9\cdot10^{-4}.
]

This looks roughly geometric in (c). But for the theorem, it is enough to prove:

[
|\mathcal K_c^{\rm off}|\to0
]

or even

[
|\mathcal K_c^{\rm off}|\le\epsilon_0
]

below the contraction margin.

Your structural explanation is good:

[
\text{off-representatives can enter only in the last }O(\log_3 c)\text{ levels}.
]

Turn this into a bound:

[
\mathcal K_c^{\rm off}\le
\sum_{j\le C\log c} 3^{-j}\cdot(\text{tail factor})
]

or whatever the exact transition count gives. The proof target should be operator-norm/off-row mass, not just total mass.

### Lemma C: heavy-state nonpersistence

This is the Perron part. The heavy row (x=-1) is the sup row, but the mass is spread:

[
\text{top column }12%,\qquad \text{top ten }43%.
]

So no single state realizes the worst case repeatedly. Prove this as a drift statement.

Using

[
\bar t_{i+1}=3\bar t_i+\bar x,
]

define a Lyapunov function such as

[
V(\bar t,\hat r)=1+\epsilon,\ell(\bar t,\hat r),
]

where (\ell) measures distance from the heavy aligned state. The goal is:

[
(\mathcal K_c V)(v)\le(1-\delta)V(v).
]

If the chain is most dangerous near (\bar t=0) and (\hat r=1), choose (V) slightly larger away from that state so that mass escaping from the heavy state receives a discount in the weighted Schur test.

Numerically, use the Perron vector of (\mathcal K_c) to guess (V). Then simplify it into a symbolic function.

## 6. Prove contraction by domination, not exact computation

A clean proof would dominate (\mathcal K_c) by a limiting positive operator:

[
\mathcal K_c\le \mathcal K_\infty+E_c,
\qquad
|E_c|\to0,
]

and prove

[
\rho(\mathcal K_\infty)<1.
]

The scaled coordinate

[
\bar t_{i+1}=3\bar t_i+\bar x
]

strongly suggests a limiting operator on a countable state space. The local weights

[
\frac13,2^{-M|\bar x|}
]

are summable in (\bar x), so (\mathcal K_\infty) should be compact or at least bounded on a weighted (\ell^\infty) space.

This may be the most elegant proof:

1. define (\mathcal K_\infty);
2. prove a weighted Schur bound
[
|\mathcal K_\infty|_V\le1-\delta;
]
3. show (\mathcal K_c\to \mathcal K_\infty) in the weighted norm.

This would avoid a long finite-(c) combinatorial proof.

## 8. What to do with (q)

The (q\le1/3) estimate should be proven separately and inserted before the cascade-contraction theorem.

The likely proof:

* In the boundary channel, the coset phase is determined by
[
\lambda\in\mathbb F_3.
]
* The (\beta=1) degenerate channel is
[
\lambda=0.
]
* Express (\lambda) as an affine nonconstant function of the leading cascade digit:
[
\lambda=A(v)\xi+B(v)\pmod3,
\qquad A(v)\ne0.
]
* Therefore exactly one of three values of (\xi) gives (\lambda=0).

This proves

[
q\le\frac13.
]

If there are exceptional states with (A(v)=0), show they are off-representative or nonrecurrent. That is probably where the heavy-state nonpersistence lemma overlaps.

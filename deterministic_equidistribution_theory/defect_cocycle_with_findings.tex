\begin{abstract}
This document merges the theoretical note proposing the deterministic defect
cocycle $\Dcoll$ for the accelerated Collatz/Syracuse map with the results of a
dedicated C numerical test suite built to probe it. Part~I states the object and
the conjectural \emph{Quenched Inverse Principle} unchanged in substance from the
original note, with the corrections suggested in review folded in as remarks.
Parts~II--IV report the suite: its construction, the per-experiment findings, and
a dedicated analysis relating the empirically detected ``shadow'' windows to the
finite transfer-operator gaps. The headline outcomes are (i) the load-bearing
$\bmod\,3^r$ congruence identity is verified to $\sim\!2.4\times10^{7}$ checks;
(ii) the finite character-sector gap $\lvert\hat p(\xi)\rvert<1$ is realized, but
the \emph{exact} coupled operator departs materially from the i.i.d.\ Fourier
shadow; and (iii) single-orbit shadows correlate with the operator gap only at
the level of the tilt (they concentrate at the critical tilt $s=1$, where the gap
is weakest), while the per-sector signal is confounded by finite residue-class
count---a concrete instance of the ``circularity leak.'' Nothing here proves
anything about Collatz; the purpose is to decide whether $\Dcoll$ is the right
finite detector and where it sees the obstruction most clearly.
\end{abstract}

\part{The object and the principle}

\section{The obstacle that fixes the shape of the object}

A single integer $x_0$ gives one deterministic valuation word
$w(x_0)=(b_0,b_1,b_2,\dots)$, where $b_i=v_2(3x_i+1)$. The annealed law is the
pushforward of Haar measure on $\Z_2$ under $x\mapsto v_2(3x+1)$. So ``the orbit
obeys the annealed law'' means: the one prescribed word $w(x_0)$ equidistributes
for the residue--height skew product. There is no ergodic theorem for a single
prescribed non-generic point, and there cannot be: ergodicity yields
almost-everywhere statements, which are silent about any individual orbit.

This dictates the shape of the missing object. It must be a \emph{dichotomy with
a closure}, not a measure-theoretic average: a pathwise quantity forced either to
decay (the orbit equidistributes) or to localize as a finite, excludable
obstruction. The quantity must reduce, at the trivial character and after
ensemble averaging, to the annealed pressure $M(s)$ and the Cram\'er tilt; and its
localization must land inside a fixed finite character sector, where
\textsf{H23a} can act.

\section{The object: a deterministic defect cocycle}

Over each finite level $(Q,L)$, each tilt $s$, and each character $\chi\ne 1$ of
$\Om_Q$, define the pathwise twisted Birkhoff functional
\[
W_n(x;\chi,s)\;=\;\frac1n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\chi(x_i\bmod Q)\,2^{s\Delta_{b_i}},
\qquad \Delta_b=\log_2 3-b .
\]
The family of these functionals, fibered over $\Qcoll$, is the object; call it the
\emph{deterministic defect cocycle} $\Dcoll$. At $s=0$ this is the residue Weyl
sum: the orbit equidistributes modulo $Q$ iff $W_n(x;\chi,0)\to0$ for every
$\chi\ne1$. The annealed pressure $M(s)$ and the critical tilt
$p^{(1)}_b=3\cdot4^{-b}$ are the $\chi=\mathbf1$, ensemble-averaged shadow of the
same functional, so $\Dcoll$ is the quenched lift of the thermodynamic formalism:
$M(s)$ is what $W_n$ self-averages to when nothing obstructs.

The content of $\Dcoll$ at finite level is one statement in three languages---%
\emph{equidistribution failure}, \emph{affine/cohomological}, and
\emph{spectral}:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\limsup_n \lvert W_n(x;\chi,s)\rvert > 0
\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;&
\chi \text{ is an approximate coboundary along the orbit}\\[2pt]
\Longleftrightarrow\;\;&
\Lcal_{s,Q,L,\chi}\text{ has a near-peripheral eigenstate read off from }\mu_n^x .
\end{aligned}
\]

\begin{remark}[Correction adopted from review]
The three-way \emph{equivalence} is too strong and is used here only in the safe
direction. A non-decaying twisted sum signals correlation with $\chi$, but need
not be an exact coboundary (it could be concentration, subsequential trapping,
nonstationarity); and producing a near-\emph{top} eigenstate of
$\Lcal_{s,Q,L,\chi}$ is precisely the open keystone, not an automatic consequence.
The load-bearing statement is the single implication: a persistent $W_n$ bias
$\Longrightarrow$ a nontrivial finite character shadow of the orbit, and
(conjecturally) a near-peripheral state of $\Lcal_{s,Q,L,\chi}$.
\end{remark}

\section{Why the annealed side has a gap, explicitly}

Use the exact affine cocycle
\[
x_n=\frac{3^n x_0+A_n}{2^{B_n}},\qquad
A_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}3^{\,n-1-j}2^{B_j},\qquad B_n=\sum_{i<n}b_i .
\]
Reducing modulo $3^r$, for $n\ge r$ the $3^n x_0$ term vanishes and only the last
$r$ valuations survive.

\begin{lemma}[Sliding-window congruence]\label{lem:cong}
For $n\ge r$,
\[
x_n \equiv \sum_{k=1}^{r} 3^{\,k-1}\, 2^{-(b_{n-1}+\cdots+b_{n-k})} \pmod{3^r}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\noindent Since $2$ is a primitive root modulo $3^r$ (order $2\cdot3^{r-1}$), the
character value $\chi_3(x_n)$ is a sliding-window function of the partial sums
$B_i\bmod 2\cdot3^{r-1}$. The single-symbol Fourier transform of the annealed law
is
\[
\hat p(\xi)=\sum_{b\ge1}2^{-b}e(b\xi)=\frac{e(\xi)}{2-e(\xi)},
\qquad
\lvert\hat p(\xi)\rvert=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5-4\cos 2\pi\xi}}<1\quad(\xi\notin\Z).
\]
The frequencies attached to a nontrivial $\chi$ are $\xi=k/(2\cdot3^{r-1})$, all
nonintegral, so to leading order in the i.i.d.\ model
$\rho(\Lcal_{0,Q,L,\chi})=\max_\xi\lvert\hat p(\xi_\chi)\rvert<1$.
\epi{Inference}; this is the i.i.d.\ value, \emph{not} the exact spectral radius
of the coupled finite operator. The distinction is enforced throughout
Parts~III--IV: \texttt{rho\_annealed} and \texttt{rho\_exact} are reported in
separate columns and never substituted.

\section{The Quenched Inverse Principle}

\begin{principlebox}{Quenched Inverse Principle (conjectural)}
Let $x_0$ have an infinite forward orbit, and suppose it violates the annealed law
persistently: for some \textbf{non-coboundary} $\chi$ on some $\Om_Q$, some tilt
$s$, and some $\delta>0$, $\lvert W_n(x_0;\chi,s)\rvert\ge\delta$ along a
positive-density set of $n$ (or, more weakly, along blocks of length
$\ell_j\to\infty$). Then there exist a finite pair $(Q,L)$ and a $\chi$-sector
eigenstate of $\Lcal_{s,Q,L,\chi}$ with
$\lvert\lambda\rvert\ge\rho(\Lcal_{s,Q,L,0})-o(1)$, exhibited by the orbit's own
empirical measure. Conjoined with the gap
$\rho(\Lcal_{s,Q,L,\chi})\le(1-\kappa_Q)\rho(\Lcal_{s,Q,L,0})$ for non-coboundary
$\chi$, this is a contradiction. Hence every integer orbit equidistributes
relative to the annealed (valuation-pushforward) residue law.
\end{principlebox}

\begin{remark}[Coboundary refinement, forced by the computations]
The qualifier ``\textbf{non-coboundary}'' is essential and is the one substantive
correction the numerics forced. The faithful operator has exactly one structural
zero-gap sector---the quadratic character $\chi_2$---and it is \emph{not} a defect
but an exact coboundary: $\chi_2(x_i)=(-1)^{b_{i-1}}$ is valuation parity in
disguise (\cref{app:quad}), so the residues are not uniform mod $3$ (they sit at
$\tfrac13:\tfrac23$) and $\chi_2$ belongs to the height/valuation \emph{reference}.
H23a is therefore not ``all nontrivial characters are gapped'' but
\[
\boxed{\ \text{all \emph{non-coboundary} characters are gapped.}\ }
\]
After quotienting the (unique) coboundary, the gap is present, $Q$-uniform, and in
fact constant (\cref{app:quad}, \cref{app:gap}). The positive-density hypothesis is
also likely too strong---obstructions may be sparse---so the principle is stated to
admit long blocks $\ell_j\to\infty$ (the form used in Exp.~I).
\end{remark}

\section{The keystone and the two leaks}

The single arrow that is not a transcription of known structure is
\[
\text{persistent quenched bias along }w(x_0)\Longrightarrow
\text{a \textbf{finite-level} approximate eigenfunction at the \textbf{top} eigenvalue.}
\]

\paragraph{Finiteness leak.} The limiting obstruction could live at
$Q\to\infty$. Closing this needs an effective, uniform gap
$\kappa_Q\gtrsim 1/\mathrm{polylog}(Q)$, decaying more slowly than the entropy
production of the bias. That quantitative \textsf{H23a} is a hypothesis.

\paragraph{Circularity leak.} Reading a top eigenfunction off $\mu_n^x$ presumes
the orbit's residue frequencies are already near-stationary---what one is trying
to prove. Breaking this needs an input not from the orbit's own statistics; the
best candidate (a regeneration-plus-bilinear engine resting on the multiplicative
independence of $2$ and $3$, with a Bourgain--Sarnak--Ziegler step over the
$3$-adic frequencies) is \epi{Speculation}, and is morally Sarnak-hard.

\section{Three falsifiable consequences (the test targets)}
\label{sec:targets}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item For each $r$, compute the exact $\rho(\Lcal_{0,3^r,L,\chi})$ and check it
tracks $\max_k(5-4\cos(2\pi k/(2\cdot3^{r-1})))^{-1/2}$. A parametrically smaller
coupled gap means the finiteness leak is real and returns the true $\kappa_Q$.
\item Along long orbits, compute the windowed $W_n(x;\chi,0)$ at resonant
frequencies; the prediction is decay with no persistent plateau. A plateau is the
spectral shadow made visible and a direct test of the circularity leak.
\item At $s=1$, compare $\rho(\Lcal_{1,3^r,L,\chi})$ with
$\rho(\Lcal_{1,3^r,L,0})$. A nontrivial gap there is the statement that a high
ascent cannot also hold a residue resonance.
\end{enumerate}

\part{The numerical test program}

\section{What was built}

The suite \texttt{collatz\_tests} (ISO~C11; profiles \textsf{FAST128} with
\texttt{\_\_uint128\_t}, \textsf{GMP} with \texttt{mpz\_t}, and a \textsf{DEBUG}
profile of invariant assertions) implements nine experiments, A--I, over the
accelerated step $S(x)=(3x+1)/2^{b}$, $b=v_2(3x+1)$. Modules cover the core
arithmetic, a streaming orbit scanner, finite characters (additive
$\chi_k(a)=e(ka/Q)$ and multiplicative on $(\Z/3^r)^\times$ with $2$ a primitive
root), the defect sums $W_{n,\ell}$ via complex prefix sums, empirical
valuation/entropy statistics, an excursion/critical-event recorder, the finite
residue--height operator builder, and a CSR sparse-matrix spectral toolkit.

Four design requirements, adopted from review, shaped the build:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt]
\item the $\bmod\,3^r$ congruence (\cref{lem:cong}) is treated as a
\emph{correctness gate} (Exp.~H), verified before any spectral code relies on it;
\item $\rho_{\mathrm{annealed}}$ and $\rho_{\mathrm{exact}}$ are kept in separate
columns and never interchanged (Exp.~F);
\item the shadow search reports per-window magnitude \emph{and} length, so both
$\limsup$ plateaus and long sparse blocks are visible---positive density is not
required (Exp.~I);
\item the quenched pressure is computed as $\limsup_n\frac1n\sum_{i<n}s\Delta_{b_i}$,
not an ill-defined sum over words.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{A definitional caveat carried throughout.}
The project-internal ``$C_A$-critical'' certificate is not restated in the source
notes. The spec's literal inequality $V^2\le 2^A P$ (launch $V$, peak $P$) is
\emph{degenerate} in practice: once the orbit descends to small record-low
launches, $V^2$ is tiny and essentially every excursion qualifies. The suite
therefore offers two readings via \texttt{--crit-mode}: \texttt{vsq} (literal) and
\texttt{height} (peak $\ge$ launch$\cdot2^A$, i.e.\ ``rises $A$ octaves''), with
\texttt{height} the default. Both are reported; the formal certificate should be
fixed before the $B/D/I$ ``critical'' labels are treated as canonical.

\part{Findings}

All runs below are from a single large sweep (\textsf{FAST128} release).

\section{Experiment H --- the congruence gate}

\begin{findingbox}{Verified}
\cref{lem:cong} holds in every test: \textbf{$23{,}905{,}968/23{,}905{,}968$}
checks match (\textsf{FAST128}, seeds $3$--$999{,}999$, $r\le8$, $0$ overflow),
plus \textbf{$144/144$} under \textsf{GMP} for a $16$-digit seed at $r=12$ with
$n$ up to $155$ (values $\gg 2^{128}$).
\end{findingbox}
\noindent The load-bearing identity is sound; downstream residue/spectral code may
rely on it. Each check compares the directly reduced orbit value $x_n\bmod 3^r$
against the sliding-window formula using $2^{-1}\bmod 3^r$.

\section{Experiment A --- height-tail census}

Pre-registration: $\log_2\Pr(h\ge H)\approx -H+C$. Over octaves $10$--$28$ at
$10^5$ seeds/octave, the per-step slope of $\log_2(\text{tail}/\text{seeds})$ at
octave $24$ averages $\approx\mathbf{-0.97}$ for $H=3,\dots,11$ (steeper in the
low-count tail). \textbf{Slope $-1$ confirmed.}

\section{Experiment B --- the Cram\'er-tilt law}

Pre-registration: the bulk valuation law of high-ascent excursions tends to
$p^{(1)}_b=3\cdot4^{-b}$ with entropy-per-digit $\to H_2(3/4)=0.8113$.

\begin{findingbox}{Cram\'er tilt confirmed (in direction)}
Octave $26$, $2\times10^5$ seeds, boundary-excluded \emph{middle} segments of
length $\ge16$ ($103{,}976$ segments):
$\hat p=(0.657,0.229,0.089,0.021)$---between annealed $(.5,.25,.125,.0625)$ and
critical $(.75,.1875,.0469,.0117)$---with
$\KL{\hat p}{p^{(1)}}=\mathbf{0.093}\ll\KL{\hat p}{p}=0.191$ and
entropy-per-digit $0.870$.
\end{findingbox}
\noindent Full segments dilute toward annealed because launch/exit boundary layers
contaminate them---precisely what the boundary-layer test isolates. Larger scale
roughly doubles the margin in favor of $p^{(1)}$ over the small run.

\section{Experiment C --- defect windows}

At $s=0$ (the residue Weyl sum) $\lvert W\rvert$ decreases with window length
($0.31$ at $L{=}16\to0.24\to0.20\to0.11$ at $L{\approx}116$ for $Q=27$), the
equidistribution direction. Single-orbit windows stay short (orbits terminate at
$1$), so the asymptotic decorrelation lives in the operator experiments E/F rather
than in single-orbit $W$.

\section{Experiment D --- post-exit taboo}

\begin{findingbox}{Entropy-space repulsion present}
Octave $24$, $6{,}496$ post-exit windows: $\KL{\hat p_{\rm post}}{p}=0.177\ll
\KL{\hat p_{\rm post}}{p^{(1)}}=0.576$, mean $M_{\rm post}(1)=0.989<1$ ($53\%$
subcritical). Post-exit relaxes toward annealed, away from the critical tilt.
\end{findingbox}
\noindent This is the proposed reason two critical excursions fail to concatenate.

\section{Experiments E and F --- the finite operator gap}

The killed residue--height operator $\Lcal_{s,Q,L,\chi}$ is built with the forward
residue map $a'=(3a+1)2^{-b}$ for odd $Q$ (so $2$ is invertible, covering
$Q=3^r$), the height update $z'=z+\lfloor R(\log_23-b)\rceil$ with killing outside
$[0,L]$, and the character phase on the source residue.

\begin{findingbox}{Target 3 (the $s=1$ gap) and Target 1 (exact vs.\ annealed)}
On the reliable \emph{residue-only} operator (Exp.~F), every nontrivial
$3^r$-sector is gapped, $\rho(\Lcal_\chi)<\rho(\Lcal_0)$, and the gap is
\emph{weakest at the critical tilt}: $s{=}1$ ratios run $0.54$--$0.93$ versus
$0.22$--$0.76$ at $s{=}0$. Against the i.i.d.\ Fourier prediction
$\lvert\hat p(\xi)\rvert$, the exact ratios deviate by $\pm0.1$--$0.36$ with
$\mathrm{corr}=\mathbf{0.38}$ and mean $\lvert\text{dev}\rvert=0.254$ across $48$
sectors.
\end{findingbox}

\begin{remark}[A solver defect surfaced by Part~IV, since fixed]\label{rem:Esolver}
The \emph{killed-height} operator (Exp.~E) at first told a different story: its
power iteration did not converge (relative residuals $3$--$9\%$; all sectors hit
the $3000$-iteration cap), because the height-transport mode produces a
near-degenerate band of top eigenvalues, and several reported gap ratios
\emph{exceeded $1$}---impossible, since with $\lvert\Lcal_\chi\rvert$ equal to the
principal operator on the same support Wielandt's theorem forces
$\rho(\Lcal_\chi)\le\rho(\Lcal_0)$. The solver was replaced by a \textbf{restarted
complex Arnoldi} (Krylov subspace, Hessenberg shifted-QR Ritz values, validated
against known spectra in the unit tests). The killed-height operator now converges
to residual $<8\times10^{-11}$ with no Wielandt violations.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The uniform gap is governed by the \emph{worst} character]
A uniform gap means $\rho(\Lcal_\chi)\le(1-\kappa_Q)\rho(\Lcal_0)$ for
\emph{all} $\chi\ne1$, so
$\kappa_Q=1-\max_\chi[\rho(\Lcal_\chi)/\rho(\Lcal_{\chi_0})]$---the
\emph{worst}-gapped character. (Computed over the full group via the conjugate
symmetry $k=1..\lfloor\phi/2\rfloor$.)
\end{remark}

\begin{findingbox}{Target 1 (the finiteness leak), full character group}
\textbf{Residue-only (the object that governs $W$): a uniform gap survives.}
$\kappa_Q=1-\max_\chi$ ratio \emph{stabilizes} as $Q$ grows---$0.236$ at
$s{=}0$, $0.074$ at $s{=}1$ for all $Q\in\{27,\dots,2187\}$---bounded well away
from $0$, with the worst character at a \emph{fixed} frequency, not $k=1$. The
lowest-frequency $k{=}1$ char, where the annealed $1-\lvert\hat p(1/\phi)\rvert$
collapses to $0$ ($0.42\to2\times10^{-5}$ as $Q\to2187$), is gapped by the
\emph{exact} operator by $0.24$--$0.78$: the exact operator gaps low frequencies
\emph{far more} than the i.i.d.\ shadow. The annealed finiteness leak is
\textbf{refuted} for the residue operator over $3^2\to3^7$.
\end{findingbox}

\subsection*{Coupling the height to the character}
The faithful \emph{skew-product} killed-height operator (one $b$ drives residue
and height) has \emph{no} uniform gap: its maximum gap ratio is \emph{exactly}
$1$, at the \textbf{quadratic character} $k=\phi/2$. This is structural, not a
discretization artifact---it stays exactly $1$ across $R\in\{2,\dots,16\}$ and
$L\in\{24,\dots,120\}$. The mechanism: the height is $\approx n\log_2 3-B_n$, so
it encodes $B_n$, and the quadratic character's $2$-adic factor
$\chi(2)^{-b}=(-1)^b$ accumulates to $(-1)^{B_n}$---a \emph{coboundary} of the
skew product, removable by a diagonal similarity for any height geometry.

\begin{findingbox}{The rebuilt coupling restores the gap}
Decoupling the synchronization---$\Lcal=\Lcal_{\mathrm{res}}(\chi)\otimes
T_{\mathrm{height}}$, the height a residue-independent survival factor, the
character twisting only the residue factor (\texttt{--height-mode factored})---%
gaps the quadratic character (ratio $0.333$ at $s{=}0$, $0.600$ at $s{=}1$, the
residue value) and gives the killed operator the residue uniform gap exactly:
$\kappa_Q=0.345\,(Q{=}9)\to0.236\,(Q\ge27)$ at $s{=}0$, matching F. The Kronecker
height factor cancels in every gap ratio, so this holds for all $Q$. The
\textbf{killed operator now inherits the stabilizing residue gap and gaps every
character}; the skew-product $\kappa_Q=0$ is isolated to the genuine
quadratic-character height-coboundary.
\end{findingbox}

\section{Experiment G --- trace and determinant shadows}

For small $(Q,L)$, $\tr(\Lcal^1)=0$, $\tr(\Lcal^2)=1.25$, $\tr(\Lcal^3)=0$,
$\tr(\Lcal^4)=0.594$, and $\det(1-z\Lcal)$ is smooth and positive
($0.95$ at $z{=}0.25\to0.82$ at $z{=}0.5$): no spurious peripheral structure.

\part{Relating the long shadow windows to the operator gaps}

This is the direct test of the keystone arrow: do shadow windows live in sectors
where the operator gap is small? The shadow moduli $\{9,27,81,243\}$ are all powers
of $3$, so multiplicative characters match between Experiments I and F, and the
join is on $(Q,\chi\text{-index},s)$. Because of \cref{rem:Esolver} the gap is
taken from the residue-only operator (F), re-run over these moduli with a
\emph{principal-on-units} baseline ($\chi_0=1$ on units, $0$ on non-units)---the
apples-to-apples trivial sector for multiplicative characters.

\subsection*{Tilt level: a clean correspondence}

\begin{findingbox}{Shadows sit at the weakest-gap tilt}
F shows residue-only gaps are systematically weaker (closer to $1$) at $s{=}1$ than
at $s{=}0$. The shadows concentrate there: of $53{,}158$ shadow windows,
$43{,}843$ ($\mathbf{82\%}$) have argmax $s{=}1$, and \textbf{all $218$ long
windows ($\ell\ge64$) are at $s{=}1$}. Long shadows also sit in weaker-gap
sectors: mean gap ratio $\mathbf{0.73}$ versus $0.57$ over all shadows.
\end{findingbox}
\noindent Both statements are the manuscript's predicted direction: a
slowly-decaying mode is permitted precisely where the gap is weak, and the
critical tilt is where the operator gap is weakest (Target~3 made dynamical).

\subsection*{Sector level: confounded by finite residue-class count}

Across sectors at fixed tilt the correspondence is weak,
$\mathrm{corr}(\lvert W\rvert,\text{gap})=+0.247$ (right sign, small). The argmax is
dominated by the \emph{smallest} modulus: $94\%$ of shadows pick $Q=9$
($49{,}727$ of $53{,}158$), which has only six unit residues, so a short window's
$\lvert\text{mean }\chi\rvert$ is large by finite-sample inflation regardless of
the asymptotic gap (the sector $Q{=}9,k{=}3$ carries $36{,}666$ shadows yet has a
healthy gap of $0.60$).

\begin{keystatement}
The finite operator decorrelates every nontrivial sector (all residue-only gaps
$<1$, weakest at $s=1$). The long shadow windows correspond to it in the predicted
direction at the level of the tilt and in gap magnitude. But the per-sector
single-orbit shadow is dominated by small-$Q$ finite-size inflation, so it is
\emph{not} clean evidence of an operator anti-gap. This is the circularity leak
made concrete: reading the obstruction off one orbit's short windows is confounded;
the operator gap is the reliable object, and it shows no anti-gap.
\end{keystatement}

\section*{Which operator powers H23a}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Which operator powers H23a}

The proof of \cref{app:quad} forces a choice of operator, and it resolves
cleanly in favour of the \textbf{faithful skew product (\texttt{sync})}, on a
\emph{coboundary-quotiented} character family. The reasoning:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \textbf{Faithfulness is non-negotiable for an inverse argument.} H23a runs
\emph{backwards}: a persistent $|W_n(x;\chi,s)|$ must force a near-peripheral
eigenstate. That implication is valid only if the operator's spectral radius
\emph{is} the growth rate of $W_n$. Along the real orbit a single valuation
$b_i$ drives both the residue step and the tilt weight $2^{s\Delta_{b_i}}$, which
is exactly the \texttt{sync} coupling. The \texttt{factored} operator draws an
independent valuation for the height, so its spectral radius does \emph{not}
govern $W_n$.

\item \textbf{The \texttt{factored} gap is a mirage.} By \cref{cor:run},
$W_n(x;\chi_2,0)=\frac1n\sum(-1)^{b_{i-1}}\to \E[(-1)^b]=-\tfrac13$ (confirmed:
mean $-0.353$ over $3.2\times10^4$ orbits). The $\chi_2$ component \emph{does not
decay}. The \texttt{factored} operator reports a gap there ($\rho<\rho_0$),
predicting decay that does not occur---a false negative that would let a genuine
non-decaying sector through. \texttt{sync} correctly returns
$\rho(\Lcal_{\chi_2})=\rho(\Lcal_{\mathbf1})$ (no decay), matching reality.

\item \textbf{The lone ungapped sector is not an obstruction---it is the
reference.} $\chi_2$ is a coboundary (\cref{thm:cobound}); its Weyl sum is
slaved to the valuation statistics, i.e.\ to the $\chi=\mathbf1$ (height/pressure)
data. Equivalently, the orbit's residues are \emph{not} uniform mod $3$: they sit
at $1:2$ ratio $\tfrac13:\tfrac23$, the pushforward of the valuation parity. So
the correct annealed residue reference is this non-uniform law, and $\chi_2$
carries no information beyond it. H23a must be posed on the quotient
$\widehat{\Om_Q}/\langle\text{coboundaries}\rangle$, where $\chi_2$ is absorbed
into the reference.

\item \textbf{On that quotient, \texttt{sync} has a positive, $Q$-uniform gap---in
fact a constant.} The worst non-coboundary character is the fixed conductor-$9$
(order-$6$) sector, gap ratio $0.9069$ for every $Q\in\{9,27,81,243\}$ and both
tilts; higher-conductor characters are strictly more gapped, and each sector's gap
is $r$-independent because the mod-$3^c$ reduction is autonomous
(\cref{prop:cond}). Hence
\[
\kappa_Q^{\text{(sync, non-coboundary)}}=\kappa_0\approx0.0931
\quad\text{constant for all }Q=3^r,\ r\ge2
\]
(\cref{thm:gapconst})---stronger than the conservative $\gtrsim1/\mathrm{polylog}(Q)$.
It is smaller than the pure residue gap $0.236$ (the height-survival mode lifts
every sector toward $1$) but bounded away from $0$, which is all H23a needs.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{keystatement}
\textbf{Decision.} Run H23a on the faithful \texttt{sync} skew-product operator,
relative to the valuation-pushforward residue reference, restricting the
character family to the coboundary-quotient. There the finite gap is
$\kappa_Q\approx0.093$, uniform in $Q$ and $s$. The \texttt{factored} and
residue-only operators are kept as \emph{diagnostics}: they isolate the pure
residue decorrelation ($\kappa=1-\tfrac{2^{1+s}-1}{2^{1+s}+1}$, stabilising at
$0.236/0.074$) and certify that $\chi_2$ is the unique difference between the two.
\end{keystatement}

\paragraph{Immediate follow-ups.}
(1)~With the operator question settled, the remaining \emph{Sync Quotient Gap
Theorem} (\cref{thm:gapconst}) has a clean proof program (\cref{app:gap}): the
only missing input is monotonicity in conductor (higher-conductor characters at
least as gapped), via complete-fiber principal-unit cancellation; combined with
the proved autonomy (\cref{prop:cond}) this yields the \emph{constant} gap
$\kappa_0\approx0.093$, with $\kappa_Q\gg1/\mathrm{polylog}(Q)$ as the fall-back.
(2)~Switch the defect-window
experiment to multiplicative characters for $3^r$ and measure per-sector
$\lvert W_{n,\ell}\rvert$ decay against the F gap rate at fixed $(Q,\chi,s)$,
controlling for $Q$. (3)~Normalize the shadow detector by residue-class count so
the per-sector signal is not swamped by small $Q$. (4)~Fix the formal
$C_A$-critical certificate so the $B/D/I$ critical labels are canonical.

The object relocates the difficulty rather than removing it. The experiments show
$\Dcoll$ behaves as advertised on the parts that are reorganizations of known
structure---the congruence, the annealed gap, the Cram\'er tilt, the post-exit
taboo, and a genuine finite operator gap weakest at the critical tilt---while the
one open arrow (a persistent quenched bias forcing a top finite-level eigenstate)
remains open, and the single-orbit shadow that would witness it is confounded by
finite size exactly as the circularity leak predicts.

\section{The quadratic-character coboundary: an analytic proof}
\label{app:quad}

This appendix proves the structural fact found numerically: for the faithful
residue--height skew product, the quadratic character $\chi_2$ of $(\Z/3^r)^\times$
is an exact coboundary, so $\rho(\Lcal_{\chi_2})=\rho(\Lcal_{\mathbf 1})$ and its
gap vanishes; whereas every character of order $>2$ stays gapped. Write
$b_i=v_2(3x_i+1)$, $B_n=\sum_{i<n}b_i$, and $S(x)=(3x+1)/2^{b}$.

\begin{lemma}[$\chi_2$ factors through reduction mod $3$]\label{lem:fac3}
For $r\ge1$, $(\Z/3^r)^\times$ is cyclic of order $\phi=2\cdot3^{r-1}$ and the
quadratic character $\chi_2$ (its unique order-$2$ character) satisfies
$\ker\chi_2=\{u:\,u\equiv1\!\!\pmod 3\}$. Equivalently $\chi_2(a)=\left(\tfrac a3\right)$,
the Legendre symbol mod $3$: $\chi_2(a)=+1$ if $a\equiv1$ and $-1$ if $a\equiv2\pmod3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$2$ is a primitive root mod $3^r$, so the group is cyclic of order $\phi$ and
$\ker\chi_2$ is its unique index-$2$ subgroup, the squares, of order $3^{r-1}$.
Reduction $(\Z/3^r)^\times\!\to(\Z/3)^\times\cong\Z/2$ has kernel
$\{u\equiv1\!\pmod3\}$, also of order $3^{r-1}$. A cyclic group has a unique
subgroup of each order, so the two coincide; hence $\chi_2$ depends only on
$a\bmod3$, equalling $+1$ on $a\equiv1$ and $-1$ on $a\equiv2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Key identity]\label{prop:key}
For every odd $x$, with $b=v_2(3x+1)$,
\[
\chi_2\!\left(S(x)\bmod 3^r\right)=(-1)^{b}.
\]
In particular the character of the image depends only on $b$, not on $x$, and
$\chi_2(x_{i+1})=(-1)^{b_i}$ along any orbit.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Work mod $3$. Since $2\equiv-1\pmod3$ and $3x+1\equiv1\pmod3$,
\[
S(x)=(3x+1)\,2^{-b}\equiv 1\cdot(-1)^{-b}\equiv(-1)^{b}\pmod 3 .
\]
Thus $S(x)\equiv1\pmod3$ iff $b$ is even and $S(x)\equiv2\pmod3$ iff $b$ is odd;
by \cref{lem:fac3}, $\chi_2(S(x))=(-1)^b$. (Note $3x+1\equiv1\pmod3$ makes $S(x)$ a
unit, so $\chi_2(S(x))\ne0$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The running character is the valuation parity]\label{cor:run}
$\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\chi_2(x_i)=(-1)^{B_n}$, and
$\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\chi_2(x_i)=\chi_2(x_0)(-1)^{B_{n-1}}$. The quadratic character
accumulated along the orbit is exactly $(-1)$ to the cumulative $2$-adic valuation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from \cref{prop:key}: $\prod_{i=1}^n\chi_2(x_i)=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{b_i}=(-1)^{B_n}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$\chi_2$ is a coboundary of the residue--valuation skew product]
\label{thm:cobound}
On the skew product with state $(a,\tau)\in(\Z/3^r)^\times\times(\Z/2)$ and
dynamics $(a,\tau)\xrightarrow{b}\big((3a+1)2^{-b},\,\tau+b\big)$, set
$d(a,\tau)=\chi_2(a)\,(-1)^{\tau}\in\{\pm1\}$. Then on every edge
\[
\frac{d(a',\tau')}{d(a,\tau)}=\chi_2(a).
\]
Consequently the twisted annealed transfer operator obeys
$\Lcal_{\chi_2}=D\,\Lcal_{\mathbf 1}\,D^{-1}$ with $D=\mathrm{diag}(d)$, so
$\rho(\Lcal_{\chi_2})=\rho(\Lcal_{\mathbf 1})$ and $\kappa=0$, for every tilt $s$
and any height discretization.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:key}, $\chi_2(a')=(-1)^b$, and $\tau'=\tau+b$ gives
$(-1)^{\tau'}=(-1)^\tau(-1)^b=(-1)^\tau\chi_2(a')$. Hence, using $\chi_2(a')^2=1$,
\[
d(a',\tau')=\chi_2(a')(-1)^{\tau'}=\chi_2(a')\,(-1)^\tau\chi_2(a')=(-1)^\tau,
\qquad
\frac{d(a',\tau')}{d(a,\tau)}=\frac{(-1)^\tau}{\chi_2(a)(-1)^\tau}=\chi_2(a).
\]
With the character carried on the source, the matrix element of $D^{-1}\Lcal_{\chi_2}D$
on this edge is $d(a',\tau')^{-1}\,w_s(b)\chi_2(a)\,d(a,\tau)=w_s(b)$, the matrix
element of $\Lcal_{\mathbf 1}$. So the operators are conjugate by the real
orthogonal $D$, hence isospectral.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why the residue alone is gapped, and the exact ratio.} Without the
$\tau$-coordinate, $\chi_2$ is \emph{not} a coboundary: the candidate potential
would be $a$-only, forcing $(-1)^b=1$. Indeed $\chi_2$ factors through mod $3$, on
which the dynamics is $\epsilon\mapsto(-1)^b$ on $\{\pm1\}$; the $2\times2$ twisted
operator $M_{\chi_2}[\epsilon',\epsilon]=\epsilon\sum_{b:(-1)^b=\epsilon'}w_s(b)$ has
eigenvalues $0$ and $\sum_b(-1)^b w_s(b)$, giving the closed form
\[
\frac{\rho(\Lcal^{\mathrm{res}}_{\chi_2})}{\rho(\Lcal^{\mathrm{res}}_{\mathbf 1})}
=\frac{\bigl|\sum_b(-1)^b w_s(b)\bigr|}{\sum_b w_s(b)}
=\frac{2^{1+s}-1}{2^{1+s}+1}
\quad\Big(=\tfrac13\text{ at }s{=}0,\ \tfrac35\text{ at }s{=}1\Big),
\]
matching Experiment F exactly. Adding $\tau$ supplies the coordinate that absorbs
the alternating sign and lifts the ratio to $1$.

\begin{remark}[Only the quadratic character]
For a character $\chi$ of order $d>2$, $\chi(3a+1)$ is \emph{not} constant: by
\cref{lem:fac3} $3a+1$ is a square, and $\chi$ restricted to the squares (order
$3^{r-1}$) is nontrivial when $d>2$. Then $\chi(a')=\chi(3a+1)\chi(2)^{-b}$ depends
on $a$, the per-edge phase is not a function of $(b,\tau)$ alone, and no diagonal
$D$ on the skew product trivialises it---so $\chi$ stays gapped. This is exactly
why the maximum gap ratio over the full group equals $1$ only at $k=\phi/2$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Height connection; \texttt{sync} vs \texttt{factored}]
The valuation parity is the height's parity: $H_n=n\log_2 3-B_n+O(1)$, so
$\tau=B_n\bmod2$ is carried by the residue--height state in the \texttt{sync}
operator (one $b$ drives residue and height together), which therefore inherits
the coboundary and shows $\kappa_Q=0$ for all $R,L$---as observed. The
\texttt{factored} operator draws the height's valuation \emph{independently} of the
residue's $b$, so $\tau$ no longer records the residue valuation, the coboundary
is broken, and the gap returns to the residue value
$1-\tfrac{2^{1+s}-1}{2^{1+s}+1}$ (and the worst-character $\kappa_Q$ stabilises).
All claims here were checked numerically: the identity of \cref{prop:key} over
$10^5$ seeds, \cref{cor:run} along every tested orbit, and
\cref{thm:cobound} by direct diagonalisation (residue-only ratio $1/3$;
residue$\times$parity ratio $1$).
\end{remark}

\section{The sync quotient gap: a constant, by conductor}
\label{app:gap}

This appendix sharpens the remaining target. After quotienting the unique
coboundary, the faithful \texttt{sync} operator has a gap that is not merely
$Q$-uniform but \emph{constant}, governed by a single fixed finite operator.

\begin{lemma}[Coboundary classification]\label{lem:cobclass}
A character $\chi$ of $U_Q=(\Z/3^r)^\times$ is a coboundary of the synchronized
residue--valuation skew product iff $\chi\in\langle\chi_2\rangle=\{1,\chi_2\}$.
\end{lemma}
\noindent\emph{Status.} The forward direction is \cref{thm:cobound}. For
uniqueness, write $U_{3^r}\cong\{\pm1\}\times C$ with $C=1+3\Z/3^r$ the principal
units (the squares $=\ker\chi_2$); since coboundaries form a group containing
$\chi_2$, it suffices to show no nontrivial character of $C$ is a coboundary. By
Wielandt's equality theorem this is equivalent to $\rho(\Lcal_{\chi})<\rho(\Lcal_0)$
for every $\chi\notin\langle\chi_2\rangle$, which the full-character-group
computation confirms for $Q\le3^4$ (every $k\ne\phi/2$ has gap ratio $<1$). An
analytic proof reduces to showing the principal-unit functional equation
$\psi(3a+1)=\psi(a^2)$ has no nontrivial solution $\psi$ on $C$ (it already fails
at $r=2$); we leave the all-$r$ statement as \cref{lem:cobclass}.

\begin{proposition}[Conductor reduction and $Q$-independence]\label{prop:cond}
For $1\le c\le r$, a character of conductor $3^c$ depends only on $a\bmod 3^c$, and
its synchronized twisted operator is carried by the reduction of the dynamics mod
$3^c$. That reduction is \emph{autonomous}: $(3a+1)\bmod 3^c$ depends only on
$a\bmod 3^{c-1}$, so $x_{i+1}\bmod 3^c$ is a function of $(x_i\bmod 3^c,\,b_i)$
alone. Hence the gap ratio of a conductor-$3^c$ sector is \textbf{independent of
$r$}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$3a\equiv 3(a\bmod 3^{c-1})\pmod{3^c}$, so $(3a+1)2^{-b}\bmod 3^c$ is determined by
$a\bmod 3^{c}$ and $b$; the induced operator on $(\Z/3^c)^\times\times(\text{height})$
makes no reference to $r$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sync quotient gap is constant]\label{thm:gapconst}
Order the non-coboundary characters by conductor. Numerically, for every
$Q=3^r$ ($r\ge2$) and every tilt $s\in[0,1]$, the gap ratio is largest at the
\emph{smallest} conductor $3^2$ and decreases with conductor:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
\text{conductor} & 3\,(\chi_2) & 9 & 27 & 81 & 243\\\hline
\text{order} & 2 & 6 & 9 & 54 & 81\\
\text{gap ratio} & 1.0000 & \mathbf{0.9069} & 0.8530 & 0.8261 & 0.7306
\end{array}
\]
each entry being $r$-independent (\cref{prop:cond}). Consequently the worst
non-coboundary sector is the fixed conductor-$9$ (order-$6$) character, and
\[
\kappa_Q^{\,\mathrm{sync}/\mathrm{cob}}
=1-0.9069=\kappa_0\approx 0.0931
\qquad\text{for all }Q=3^r,\ r\ge2,
\]
a \emph{constant}, not merely $\gtrsim 1/\mathrm{polylog}(Q)$.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\emph{Status.} \cref{prop:cond} (each conductor's gap is $r$-independent)
is proved; the value $\kappa_0$ is the gap of the fixed conductor-$9$ operator (a
small matrix, exactly computable). The remaining input is monotonicity in
conductor---that higher-conductor characters are at least as gapped---which the
data show cleanly and which is the content of the cancellation step below.

\paragraph{Proof program for the gap theorem.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Coboundary classification} (\cref{lem:cobclass}): $\langle\chi_2\rangle$
is the only character coboundary; reduces to a principal-unit functional equation.
\item \textbf{Principal-unit cancellation:} for each non-coboundary $\chi$, a
finite admissible block from every bulk state carries untwisted mass $\ge\alpha$
on which $\lvert\E[\chi(\text{endpoint})]\rvert\le 1-\eta$. Where the block images
contain a full coset of a subgroup $H$ with $\chi|_H\ne1$, cancellation is
\emph{exact} ($\sum_{u\in H}\chi(uh)=0$)---the likely source of the flat,
conductor-monotone gap.
\item \textbf{Conductor reduction} (\cref{prop:cond}): each sector's gap is a fixed
$r$-independent number; the extremal one is conductor $9$.
\item \textbf{Height/QSD transfer:} the synchronized height weights on the paired
cancelling blocks are comparable ($w_s(w_1)/w_s(w_2)\in[C^{-1},C]$ uniformly for
$s\in[0,1]$) and the blocks stay in the QSD bulk up to $O(e^{-cL})$, so the
cancellation survives killing.
\end{enumerate}
Together these give $\kappa_Q\ge\kappa_0>0$; the conservative fall-back target is
$\kappa_Q\gg1/\mathrm{polylog}(Q)$.

\begin{remark}[What the numerics now mean]
The flatness of $\kappa_Q$ through $Q=3^5$ and the worst frequency at $\xi=1/6$
say the extremal obstruction is \emph{bounded-conductor} (conductor $9$), not a
high-conductor random walk whose gap would decay like $Q^{-c}$. High-conductor
principal-unit characters are killed strongly (apparently by near-complete-fiber
averaging), leaving a single fixed low-level sector---hence a true constant gap.
\end{remark}

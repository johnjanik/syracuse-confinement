\begin{remark}[Status of these notes]
This note builds on the pro-object $\Qcoll$ of the previous note (the quenched
residue-height spectral shadow) and proposes the object that would make its central
inverse arrow a theorem rather than a slogan. The construction is a reorganization of
known finite-level structure in three places, marked as \epi{Inference}, around one arrow
that is genuinely open, marked as \epi{Speculation}. The \emph{Quenched Inverse Principle}
is conjectural. Project-internal terms (\textsf{H23a}, the QSD machinery, the residue
spaces $\Om_Q$, the tilted operators $\Lcal_{s,Q,L,\chi}$) are carried over without
restatement. Throughout, $e(t) = e^{2\pi i t}$. Nothing here has been verified.
\end{remark}

\section{The obstacle that fixes the shape of the object}

A single integer $x_0$ gives one deterministic valuation word
$w(x_0) = (b_0, b_1, b_2, \dots)$, where $b_i = v_2(3x_i + 1)$. The annealed law is the
pushforward of Haar measure on $\Z_2$ under $x \mapsto v_2(3x+1)$. So ``the orbit obeys the
annealed law'' means: the one prescribed word $w(x_0)$ equidistributes for the
residue-height skew product. There is no ergodic theorem for a single prescribed
non-generic point, and there cannot be: ergodicity yields almost-everywhere statements,
which are silent about any individual orbit.

This dictates the shape of the missing object. It must be a \emph{dichotomy with a
closure}, not a measure-theoretic average: a pathwise quantity that is forced either to
decay (the orbit equidistributes) or to localize as a finite, excludable obstruction. The
quantity must reduce, at the trivial character and after ensemble averaging, to the
annealed pressure $M(s)$ and the Cram\'er tilt; and its localization must land inside a
fixed finite character sector, where \textsf{H23a} can act.

\section{The object: a deterministic defect cocycle}

Over each finite level $(Q,L)$, each tilt $s$, and each character $\chi\ne 1$ of $\Om_Q$,
define the pathwise twisted Birkhoff functional
\[
W_n(x;\chi,s) \;=\; \frac1n \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \chi(x_i \bmod Q)\, 2^{s\Delta_{b_i}},
\qquad \Delta_b = \log_2 3 - b.
\]
The family of these functionals, fibered over $\Qcoll$, is the object; call it the
\emph{deterministic defect cocycle} $\Dcoll$.

At $s=0$ this is the residue Weyl sum: the orbit equidistributes modulo $Q$ if and only if
$W_n(x;\chi,0)\to 0$ for every $\chi\ne 1$. The annealed pressure $M(s)$ and the critical
tilt $p^{(1)}_b = 3\cdot 4^{-b}$ are the $\chi=\mathbf 1$, ensemble-averaged shadow of the
same functional. So $\Dcoll$ is the quenched lift of the thermodynamic formalism: $M(s)$ is
what $W_n$ self-averages to when nothing obstructs.

The content of $\Dcoll$ is a single identity, written in three languages, true at finite
level and checkable:
\[
\begin{aligned}
        &\limsup_n \lvert W_n(x;\chi,s)\rvert > 0
        &&(\text{equidistribution failure})\\[2pt]
\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        &\chi \text{ is an approximate coboundary along the orbit}
        &&(\text{affine / cohomological})\\[2pt]
\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        &\Lcal_{s,Q,L,\chi} \text{ has a near-peripheral eigenstate read off from } \mu_n^x
        &&(\text{spectral}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The first equivalence is elementary: a twisted Birkhoff sum that does not decay telescopes
precisely when the character is a near-coboundary along the orbit. The second is
Perron--Frobenius duality: any weak-$\ast$ limit of the empirical measures $\mu_n^x$ is
invariant up to the telescoping error $\tfrac1n(\delta_{x_n} - \delta_{x_0}) = O(1/n)$, and
a nonzero $\chi$-component of an invariant measure is exactly a peripheral eigenstate of the
twisted operator. \epi{Inference}; this is standard for finite-state transfer operators,
transcribed onto the operators $\Lcal_{s,Q,L,\chi}$.

\section{Why the annealed side has a gap, explicitly}

At finite level the gap is computable. Use the exact affine cocycle
\[
x_n = \frac{3^n x_0 + \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} 3^{\,n-1-j}\, 2^{B_j}}{2^{B_n}},
\qquad B_j = \sum_{i<j} b_i .
\]
Reduce modulo $3^r$. For $n\ge r$ the $3^n$ term vanishes and only the last $r$ valuations
survive:
\[
x_n \equiv \sum_{k=1}^{r} 3^{\,k-1}\, 2^{-(b_{n-1} + \cdots + b_{n-k})} \pmod{3^r}.
\]
Since $2$ is a primitive root modulo $3^r$ (order $2\cdot 3^{r-1}$), the character value
$\chi_3(x_n)$ is a sliding-window function of the partial sums $B_i \bmod 2\cdot 3^{r-1}$.
The single-symbol Fourier transform of the annealed law is
\[
\hat p(\xi) = \sum_{b\ge1} 2^{-b}\, e(b\xi) = \frac{e(\xi)}{2 - e(\xi)},
\qquad
\lvert \hat p(\xi)\rvert = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5 - 4\cos 2\pi\xi}} < 1
\quad (\xi\notin\Z).
\]
The frequencies attached to a nontrivial $\chi$ are $\xi = k/(2\cdot 3^{r-1})$, all
nonintegral, so $\lvert\hat p(\xi)\rvert < 1$ at each. To leading order in the
i.i.d.\ model,
\[
\rho\bigl(\Lcal_{0,Q,L,\chi}\bigr) = \max_\xi \lvert\hat p(\xi_\chi)\rvert < 1.
\]
\epi{Inference}; this is the leading-order i.i.d.\ value, not the exact spectral radius of
the coupled finite operator, but it pins the mechanism and the gap constant $\kappa_Q$.
That explicit strict inequality is \textsf{H23a} made arithmetic: nontrivial characters
decorrelate because $5 - 4\cos 2\pi\xi > 1$ off the integers.

\section{The Quenched Inverse Principle}

\begin{principlebox}{Quenched Inverse Principle (conjectural)}
Let $x_0$ have an infinite forward orbit, and suppose it violates the annealed law
persistently: for some $\chi\ne 1$ on some $\Om_Q$, some tilt $s$, and some $\delta>0$,
\[
\lvert W_n(x_0;\chi,s)\rvert \ge \delta
\quad\text{along a positive-density set of } n .
\]
(This covers the three concrete failures: a sub-equilibrium tail $\bar b_n < \log_2 3$;
persistent high-ascent shadowing $\KL{\mu_n}{p^{(1)}}\to 0$; and residue bias.) Then there
exist a finite pair $(Q,L)$ and a $\chi$-sector eigenstate of $\Lcal_{s,Q,L,\chi}$ with
eigenvalue
\[
\lvert\lambda\rvert \ge \rho\bigl(\Lcal_{s,Q,L,0}\bigr) - o(1),
\]
exhibited by the orbit's own empirical measure. Conjoined with the gap
$\rho(\Lcal_{s,Q,L,\chi}) \le (1-\kappa_Q)\rho(\Lcal_{s,Q,L,0})$, this is a contradiction.
Hence every integer orbit equidistributes and obeys the annealed law.
\end{principlebox}

The contradiction chain is the one stated before: bad orbit $\Rightarrow$ finite
spectral/coboundary shadow $\Rightarrow$ violation of \textsf{H23a}. The role of $\Dcoll$ is
to make the first arrow a theorem-shaped statement.

\section{The keystone, where the real theorem lives}

Everything above is reorganization. The single arrow that is not a transcription of known
structure is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{persistent quenched bias along } w(x_0)\\
\Longrightarrow\quad &\text{a \textbf{finite-level} approximate eigenfunction at the \textbf{top} eigenvalue}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The empirical-measure argument supplies an invariant functional, but invariance together
with nonzero $\chi$-mass does not by itself localize the obstruction at bounded $Q$ with
eigenvalue pushed all the way to the principal one. Two leaks must be closed, and naming
them is the useful part.

\paragraph{Finiteness leak.} The limiting obstruction could in principle live at
$Q\to\infty$, an obstruction that recedes to the boundary and never appears at any fixed
modulus. Closing this requires an effective, uniform gap,
\[
\kappa_Q \gtrsim \frac{1}{\mathrm{polylog}(Q)},
\]
decaying more slowly than the entropy production of the bias. That quantitative
\textsf{H23a} is a hypothesis, not a consequence; it is the precise point where
``annealed-to-quenched'' becomes a rate inequality.

\paragraph{Circularity leak.} Reading a top-eigenvalue eigenfunction off $\mu_n^x$
presumes the orbit's residue frequencies are already near-stationary, which is what one is
trying to prove. Breaking this requires an input that does not come from the orbit's own
statistics.

My best candidate for the circularity-breaking input, pressed as far as I will commit, is a
regeneration-plus-bilinear engine resting on the multiplicative independence of $2$ and $3$.
The valuation word is $2$-adic data; the residue modulo $3^r$ is $3$-adic data; the map
$3x+1$ couples them. Open the Weyl sum bilinearly across the two scales and apply a
Bourgain--Sarnak--Ziegler orthogonality step over the $3$-adic frequencies, using that the
$2$-adic increments at distinct $3$-adic frequencies are jointly nonresonant
($\lvert\hat p(\xi)\rvert < 1$ at every $\xi\in\frac{1}{2\cdot 3^{r-1}}\Z\setminus\Z$). The
regeneration times are the indices where the height walk hits fresh record lows, which
re-uniformize the residue; between regenerations the increments are independent enough for a
quenched Borel--Cantelli, summable because the bad-stretch probability is $\le e^{-c\ell}$
on a stretch of length $\ell$. \epi{Speculation}, on the structure of a closure; no such
argument exists, and a Bourgain--Sarnak--Ziegler step on a noninvertible monoid with
arithmetically determined inputs is exactly the unsolved part.

The honest counterweight: that keystone is morally a normality / disjointness statement for
the $2$-adic word of the integers, and it is Sarnak-hard. The deterministic word $w(x_0)$ is
zero-entropy data attached to one integer, and demanding that it decorrelate from the
$3$-adic character system for every $x_0$ has the same flavor of difficulty as M\"obius
disjointness for low-complexity sequences. So $\Dcoll$ does not dissolve Collatz; it
relocates the difficulty into one named, falsifiable lemma and gives that lemma an exact
finite-level target.

\section{Making ``annealed-to-quenched'' literal}

The statistical-mechanics gloss sharpens the prediction rather than decorating it. The
annealed pressure is $\log M(s)$. The quenched pressure along an orbit is
\[
\Lambda^{\que}(s;x_0) = \lim_{n} \frac1n \log \sum_{w_n} (\text{tilted weights along the orbit}).
\]
Quenched equal to annealed is self-averaging of the free energy. A bad orbit is a
self-averaging failure, the Collatz analogue of replica-symmetry breaking, and the inverse
principle asserts that every such failure is carried by a finite character sector. So the
conjecture reads, concretely:

\begin{keystatement}
The quenched Collatz pressure self-averages with no replica-symmetry breaking, and any
RSB component is a peripheral eigenstate of some $\Lcal_{s,Q,L,\chi}$ with $\chi\ne 1$.
\end{keystatement}
\epi{Inference} for the framing; \epi{Speculation} that no-RSB holds.

\section{Three falsifiable consequences computable now}

These would weaken or strengthen the object without waiting on the keystone.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item For each $r$, compute the exact $\rho(\Lcal_{0,3^r,L,\chi})$ and check that it tracks
\[
\max_\xi \lvert\hat p(\xi)\rvert
= \max_k \bigl(5 - 4\cos(2\pi k/(2\cdot 3^{r-1}))\bigr)^{-1/2}.
\]
If the coupled operator's gap is parametrically smaller than the i.i.d.\ value, the
finiteness leak is real, and the computation returns the true rate of $\kappa_Q$.

\item Along long orbits, compute the windowed Weyl sums $W_n(x;\chi,0)$ at the resonant
frequencies $k/(2\cdot 3^{r-1})$. The prediction is decay at rate
$\lvert\hat p(\xi)\rvert^{\,n}$ with no persistent plateau. A plateau at any fixed $\chi$
for any orbit is the spectral shadow made visible, and a direct test of the circularity
leak.

\item At $s=1$, compare $\rho(\Lcal_{1,3^r,L,\chi})$ with $\rho(\Lcal_{1,3^r,L,0})$. A
nontrivial gap there is the statement that a high ascent cannot also hold a residue
resonance, which is the joint annealed-to-quenched obstruction for critical excursions.
\end{enumerate}
